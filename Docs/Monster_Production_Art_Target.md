# Monster production art target

User direction: refine every humanoid enemy; preserve the six approved slimes. The prior model pass is rejected as too rudimentary. A working export or rig is not visual acceptance.

Latest art direction: Korean magical adventure/action manhwa, with Solo Leveling as a tonal reference. Shift the humanoids toward sharp predatory features, darker iron/leather, muted skin tones, amber eyes and blue/violet mana accents. Keep original Isekai Village creature designs and the existing class roster. This supersedes the earlier cheerful humanoid palette. The slimes remain unchanged.

Reference research: [official episode 17 gallery](https://sololeveling-anime.net/story/?id=17), [official episode 18 gallery](https://sololeveling-anime.net/story/?id=18), and [licensed distributor's monster guide](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/guides/2024/3/30/solo-leveling-monsters-magic-beasts). These inform tone and enemy visual vocabulary; their images are not imported into the game's textures or geometry.

## Reusable instruction

Create a production-quality character-art rebuild for the skeleton, goblin and ogre families. Match the approved concept sheets and the existing BodyA player's anime art direction in actual Unreal renders. Treat the existing procedural humanoid models as reference proxies. Preserve the approved slimes. Establish a close-up-ready skeleton and creature anatomy benchmark, then carry that quality across every class. Require convincing anatomy, distinctive creature faces, tailored clothing, shaped armor, coherent painted textures, clean deformation, and readable silhouettes from front, rear and three-quarter views. Do not declare the roster finished based on asset counts, polygon counts or successful import. Report art gaps honestly and retain editable sources.

## Reference and acceptance

Primary references: ArtSource/Monsters/Concepts/Skeleton_Classes_v01.png, Goblin_Classes_v01.png, Ogre_Classes_v01.png and BodyA_Reference.png. The printed lore is concept interpretation; the requested roster and user's instructions control scope.

- Skeletons: anatomical bone transitions, shaped orbital/nasal cavities, restrained soul lights, a believable jaw, smooth rib arcs, and class-specific equipment. Avoid repeated cylindrical bones, round bubble armor and a basic robe cylinder.
- Goblins: sharp sloping brow, expressive amber eyes, pointed fleshy ears, distinctive nose/jaw, compact agile anatomy, and tailored class gear. A green human with attachments is insufficient.
- Ogres: massive torso/forearms, broad creature jaw, integrated tusks, fitted hair, and materially heavier weapons/armor. Avoid floating hair, face attachments and exaggerated inflated shoulder balls.
- Surfaces: skin shading in the same family as BodyA, controlled anime tonal shapes, distinct bone/leather/cloth/metal response, restrained wear and coherent texture scale. Fine random noise does not constitute painted texture quality.
- Technical finish: clean neck transitions, no visible gear/body penetration at rest or through representative motion, natural hands on weapons, plausible foot contact, consistent texel density, usable LODs and correct material assignments.
- Review: native Unreal renders at gameplay distance and close-up, including sides/back, neutral and world lighting, and motion samples. Beauty illustrations are design references, not evidence that game meshes meet the target.

The v02 pass improves the editable geometry and equipment, and uses the installed player's material/detail maps for humanoid skin. It is still a procedural refinement. Bespoke sculpting, dedicated retopology where proportions change, unique painted texture/bake work, hair refinement, animation polish and a sustained character-art review are necessary before these can be honestly described as finished AAA-quality enemies.
